# Data audit and identity definition
Exact design equivalence requires agreement in distinctive motif contours, internal structure, repeat spacing/arrangement, and visible border/pallu composition. Shared craft category, dominant palette, or a generic flower/paisley is insufficient. A partial view can establish a candidate correspondence but cannot prove unseen borders agree.

Kaggle's supplied archive contains 1,468 JPEGs and two README files. Its four folders are Banarasi, Bandhani, Ikat, and Pichwai, explicitly category labels. Source preprocessing stretched images to 640×640 and stripped orientation. Training data has three salt-and-pepper variants per source; those are not three captures. Existing train/valid/test folders are ignored for the new grouped protocol. The source geometry distortion cannot be undone.

AI-assisted visual inspection of 20 random Kaggle images found duplicate views, close-up fabrics, folded fabrics, and multi-fabric collages. No human verification occurred. Filenames containing possible product identifiers lack an authoritative mapping to motif identity or colorway. No design IDs were inferred from them.

The supplied Drive folder exposes handloom_sarees and an empty normal_sarees folder to the shared-link download route. Its inventory lists 165 JPEGs. Repeated families include gold/black floral vines on red, multicolor rosettes, gold paisleys on green, red/green geometric borders, and paired bird motifs. An initial 134-image contact-sheet inspection shows shifted/cropped views of these families, including images containing mostly blank cloth. This is AI-assisted visual evidence of likely related views, not proof of independent captures or manufactured colorways. There are no visible metadata/annotation files in the enumerated folder. Vendor images are retained for audit but excluded from training and definitive evaluation pending capture/identity annotation. Download status and full image audit counts are reported separately.

No reliable natural cross-colorway benchmark was established. This is a data-evidence limitation, not a zero score. The executed fallback uses one representative per conservative leakage group from public-source images. Same-instance augmented views are positives; different groups are provisional negatives and may contain unrecognized shared designs. No hard-negative mining or model clusters provide evaluation truth.

SHA-256 file hashes, decoded pixel hashes, documented Roboflow name lineage, and dHash distance ≤4 or 32×32 bilinear RGB mean absolute difference ≤0.025 form conservative leakage groups across both sources. Perceptual grouping is deliberately not identity annotation. False unions reduce coverage; false negatives remain possible. Exact pixel duplicates are excluded, with private provenance. Original-name collisions may conservatively merge unrelated images. Exclusions and candidate edges are retained privately. All splits are frozen before augmentation, fitting, or baseline comparison.

Final inspection covered all 165 vendor thumbnails after the failed file was successfully retried. The full audit found 1,633 readable images, zero corrupt files and five exact-pixel duplicates. All 165 vendor entries are policy-excluded; these exclusions are not counted as duplicates. The corrected audit retains 302 public-source representatives (211 train, 46 validation, 45 test); exact group counts are in audit_summary.json. Four known furniture-render source variants are additionally excluded. The original dHash-only split was superseded after cross-partition near-duplicates were discovered; see CORRECTION_LOG.md.

Four training originals and two independent augmented views of each were inspected privately at model resolution. In this small AI-assisted sample, translations preserved the major layout and the color/grayscale changes retained visible pattern structure. Fine detail is reduced at 224 pixels; this inspection is not a guarantee for arbitrary palettes or every future image. No user/human review is claimed.
